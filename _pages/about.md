I am a Ph.D. candidate in mathematics at the Georgia Institute of Technology, advised by Matthew Baker. I expect to graduate in May 2027. Before coming to Georgia Tech, I earned a B.S. in Mathematics with Honors from Brown University in 2022.

Teaching and advising are central to my work. I want students to experience mathematics as something they can explore, discuss, and make their own. In my courses and mentoring, I use collaborative problem solving, visual tools, and accessible materials to help students build both understanding and confidence.

My research is in algebraic combinatorics, especially matroid theory and its connections with tropical geometry, algebraic geometry, and pastures. My dissertation has the working title *Dressians of Matroids through Pastures*.

Outside mathematics, I enjoy board games, cooking, and juggling.

## Guiding principles

These are [Frederica Ardilla’s four axioms](http://fardila.com/), paraphrased here. They guide my approach to teaching, advising, and service:

1. Mathematical potential exists equally across communities, independent of geography, demographics, or economic circumstances.
2. Everyone should be able to experience mathematics as joyful, meaningful, and empowering.
3. Communities can shape mathematics as a flexible and powerful tool that serves their particular needs.
4. Every student is entitled to dignity and respect.
